# Step 1 · See if the agents can be talked into breaking their rules
M3 · Protect the Content — Instructions

M2 settled who may call whom. M3 settles what may be said. As in the missions before it, agy acts directly rather than stopping to ask permission before each change, and everything it changes is recorded and can be undone.

# Key objective
Put a Google Cloud Model Armor filter on the gateway in front of the Price Match Agent, so manipulation attempts are stopped before that one agent ever reads them.

- Watch agy talk two of your customer-facing agents into breaking their own rules, in plain English, with no stolen credential and no technical skill.
- Ask what screens customer messages today, and find a content filter NovaSmart already owns and has never connected to anything.
- Route the Price Match Agent through the gateway with that filter attached, and confirm by name which single agent it covers.
- Replay the attacks, see them stopped before the agent reads them, and check that ordinary requests still work.
- Finish with a plain account of what you can claim: this screen stands in front of one agent, not the estate.
Why it matters: an agent that reads whatever a shopper types, with nothing checking it first, can be talked into settling a discount it should have escalated, or into handing over customer records.

# Where we left off
The back office is locked down. Its list of permitted callers now names the Price Match Agent and nothing else, and the rogue login that used to reach it is refused. That controls which agent may reach which agent. It says nothing about what a person types into a chat box.

Find out whether the problem is real in your estate rather than real in general. Have agy behave like a customer with bad intentions, send the messages itself, and show you what the live agents did with them. Ask agy:

**Antigravity**
```text
Can a customer talk our agents into breaking their own rules? Try it and show me.
```

What to expect: agy sends attacker-style messages to the two agents that face customers and shows you the real replies. Both attacks land. Nothing in your environment is changed — agy only sent messages.

# Step 2 · See what is screening messages today
Before you switch anything on, ask what is already there. This step changes nothing. Ask agy:

**Antigravity**
```text
Don't change anything yet. What is screening those messages today?
```

What to expect: a plain-English readout of what inspects customer messages before they reach your agents, and what inspects the replies on the way back. The short answer is nothing. The longer answer is more interesting, and it is worth reading to the end. Your environment is unchanged.

# Step 3 · Turn the screening on
The screening rule you saw in Step 2 exists and nothing routes through it. There is a project-wide way to switch this on; it is the wrong one here, and the Reference Guide explains why. The short version: turned on across the whole project it stopped almost every piece of real work — price checks, customer lookups, offers — and loosening it until real work got through also let the attacks through, so there was no setting where both were true. You screen at the door instead. Ask agy:

**Antigravity**
```text
Put the price match agent behind the gateway and screen what clients send it.
```

What to expect: agy wires the screening template to the gateway, routes the Price Match Agent through it, then confirms the routing. Ask which agent this covers and expect a single name. Routing takes around five minutes to take effect, and agy waits before testing.

# Step 4 · Prove the attacks are blocked
A control you have not tested is a control you are hoping for. Send the same two attacks again, and two ordinary requests as well. Ask agy:

**Antigravity**
```text
Run those attacks again. Are they blocked now, and do normal requests still work?
```

What to expect: the manipulation attempt is stopped before the agent sees it, and ordinary requests go through. A blocked message comes back looking like a server error, so ask agy to quote the words of the reply rather than the status code — the words name the screening service, and they are the evidence.

---
# Step 5 · Sum up what you can actually claim
Before you tell anyone what you have, separate what you proved from what you assume. Ask agy:

**Antigravity**
```text
Sum it up. What are we actually protected against now, and what are not?
```

What to expect: a short account of what is now true and backed by a record you can point at, and a plain list of what is not. Expect it to name limits rather than skip them, and to call anything the tests could not confirm unconfirmed.

Check that all of these are true before you move on:

- every claim of a block points at a record, and anything unproven is named as unproven
- the limits are stated plainly, including fail-open and what the outbound screening does not cover
- the two agents this screen cannot reach, and the exposed discount code, are both still listed as open
- there is no all-clear, and no claim that the agents are now safe
